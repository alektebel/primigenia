# Primigenia: planet canon (merged 2026-11-18 by Opus)

Backbone chosen by the plausibility referee and the peer reviews: the atmosphere and sensory consequences of draft A (GLM, senses) and
the star, moon, geology and evening planet of draft C (DeepSeek, skeptic), with the referee's fixes. Cite facts by id.

## Star and orbit
- **F1** Star: a quiet G1V, yellow-white, 1.05 L☉, 3.8 Gyr old; UV 1.15× the Sun's. The art fixes a yellow-white sun and a blue sky.
- **F10** Flares: C-class about monthly, X-class about once per 40 years (an old G dwarf is quiet). A flare is a rare world event: auroras, UV pulses at altitude.
- **F11** Orbit 1.02 AU; year 367 Earth days = 339 local days; axial tilt 26.5°, eccentricity 0.02: four seasons of ~85 local days, a bit stronger than Earth's.
- **F12** An inner sibling planet at 0.62 AU, a cloudy super-Earth: "the evening star". Being inside our orbit it is only seen near the setting or rising sun. (The faint line from the setting sun to it is the Petrova wink, A20.)

## Planet
- **F2** Gravity 1.05 g (R 1.04 R⊕, M 1.14 M⊕). Slightly heavier than Earth: falls and cliffs matter; giants are allowed by oxygen, not by low gravity.
- **F3** Day 26 h; twilight ~38 min (thick air scatters more), long dawns and dusks.
- **F6** One large moon: 1.2 Lunar masses at 450,000 km; month 29.7 d; spring tides every 14.9 d. Moonlit nights are bright; moonless nights are truly dark.
- **F13** Tides 0.4 m in open ocean but ~3.2 m on the island's shelf (shelf resonance, Bay of Fundy-style but milder): at spring low tide a causeway to the islet opens.
- **F14** Magnetic field 1.8× Earth's; surface UV 0.9× Earth's at sea level (more ozone in the thick air) but 1.4× at 2,000 m: the high slopes are a UV hazard.

## Atmosphere and climate
- **F4** Dense air: 1.6 bar. Sound speed stays ~345 m/s (it depends on temperature, not pressure), but low frequencies carry 1.6–2× farther on still, foggy dawns (temperature inversions duct them along the ground); high pitches die within tens of metres in fog and grass. Drag is 1.6× Earth's: terminal velocity ~25% lower, gliding cheap, sprinting costly.
- **F5** Oxygen 21% → pO₂ ≈ 0.34 bar (1.6× Earth's). Large aerobic bodies and costly organs (glow, sonar, spines) are affordable. Fuels stay wet, so lightning fires are rare: fire is a drought-year event.
- **F15** Composition N₂ ~77%, O₂ 21%, Ar 1.5%, CO₂ ~0.1%, humid. Mean sea-level temperature 14 °C; lapse 6.2 K/km; snow above ~1,750 m in winter and in shaded gullies all year.
- **F16** Smell: molecular diffusion ~0.6× Earth's, so in still air scent trails last ~1.6× longer; in wind the advantage disappears. Scent is a dawn-and-fog sense.
- **F17** Sky: deep blue zenith, milky horizon, copper sunsets (1.6× Rayleigh depth).
- **F8** Fog: cold upwelling sea on the west coast meets warm volcanic ground; the east start valley is a fog trap about 180 mornings a year. Evening convection storms on the 26 h day; lightning is frequent.
- **F18** Rain shadow: westerlies make the west coast wet and forested; the east meadows (the start valley) are drier in summer, with a spring.
- **F19** Wind force per m/s is 1.6× Earth's: gales in winter push light creatures and favour low, heavy bodies on exposed ridges.

## Ocean, geology, chemistry
- **F20** Seas: salinity ~3.8%, carcasses float and drift ashore; the shallows are turbid (visibility under 10 m) from volcanic sediment and plankton, so sea creatures rely on pressure sense, touch and electric sense more than sight.
- **F7** The island is a hotspot stratovolcano, 1.2 Myr old, ~2,050 m high, visible top 3.6 km across over an 8 km submarine base. Last eruption ~400 years ago: dormant, with fumaroles and hot springs.
- **F9** Rock: andesite makes steep, unstable cliffs; basalt makes lava tubes (the caves). The sunken cave is a lava tube flooded by the sea; the hidden cave is a dry upper tube; the waterfall cave is an overhang in soft tuff.
- **F21** The rock spire is a hard dyke left standing by erosion; the giant tree grows at the spring where that dyke dams groundwater. Sea arches and islets are dykes and drowned cones the sea has not yet cut.
- **F22** Two arrivals. The FIRST seed capsule fell into this world's ocean ~900 million years ago and started all life (the cell stage). The meteor crater on the island is the SECOND arrival: 600 m wide, 25 m rim, ~12,000 years old, in cohesive tuff; a ~40 m iron-nickel body carrying a second, smaller capsule (the makers' follow-up). The 5 shards lie along one bent ejecta ray. What the second capsule did is the stage's mystery (it ends at the crater with cognition).
- **F23** Minerals creatures eat: salt from spray and seeps, calcium from shell beds and bone, basalt/olivine grit (gastroliths), kaolin clay (gut detox), sulfur at fumaroles. Poisons: arsenic and mercury in hot-spring crusts, SO₂ and H₂S near vents and in stagnant cave pools, copper in green crusts. Mineral licks are fixed points: migration corridors and ambush sites.

## Life's chemistry
- **F24** One biochemistry from the seed capsule: a porphyrin photosystem, DNA-like heredity, L-amino acids, a nitrogen-fixing symbiont. Any carcass can feed any creature, and parts can graft between lineages.
- **F25** Plants are green by ancestry (the seed's pigment), not because green is optimal. Under strong UV (high slopes) and in poisonous plants they paint red-purple pigments over the green: leaf colour is an honest, readable signal of altitude stress and of alkaloids.
- **F26** Food: sugars, starchy fruit, flesh, chitin, bone, salt. Poison: alkaloid plants (red-veined), H₂S pools, raw sulfur (poison to eat, fuel for the fire-breathing lineage).
- **F27** The island's life is an island radiation: the island rose 1.2 Myr ago (F7) and was colonised by a few founders that rafted, swam or flew from the mainland, then split into many species (like Hawaiian honeycreepers or Galápagos finches on Earth). So cousins are recognisable, convergent solutions are everywhere, giants and dwarfs appear (island rules), and some niches are still empty (room for new species).

## Feel in play (referee's pick)
1. Sound is terrain: low calls travel, high calls die; fog mornings are for ears and noses, clear middays for eyes.
2. The day has a schedule: dawn fog, clear midday, evening storms, bright or truly dark nights by moon phase, a tide that opens the islet.
3. Heavy air: chases are short and costly, falls are survivable, gliding is cheap.
