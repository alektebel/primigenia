# Befriend on 4 bodies, 4 actions each (A77 step 2)

Open [befriend_bodies.html](../../../web/gaitlab/befriend_bodies.html) with `file://`. One page, a **4x4 grid**: rows SING, DANCE, CHARM, POSE; columns Armed, Winged, Octopus, Snake-like. Code: `bb_core.js` (maths, ring tubes, the Zanco-like target), one rig file per body (`bb_armed.js`, `bb_bird.js`, `bb_octo.js`, `bb_snake.js`), `bb_check.js` (self-check). Every cell shows the performer, the friendly target 2.0 to 2.6 m away, the label `organ: <organ> - tier N`, a beat dial and the timing bar of the game (beat 1.2 s, window +-0.2 s green, white tick = beat, gold = success frame). Controls: 1x / 0.5x / 0.25x, frame step, loop, cameras (game / side / front 3/4), `T` frame (close on performer = default, both, close on target), star per cell, **Copy choices** gives `armed: sing=trill-chest; dance=stomp-clap; ... | bird: ...` (localStorage in try/catch), click or `F` enlarges a cell (the description appears). `?test=1` pauses at t=0; `window.__stylesCheck` holds the self-check.

Framing rule from the pose-page lesson: close by default, the performer fills 45 to 65 % of the cell height; the target is partly in frame (head and neck, front legs, the gold ring). The performer is turned 0.55 rad (about 30 degrees) towards the game camera so frontal displays (frill, hood, throat sac, chest) read; the target stays straight ahead. This is a display angle, not a bug.

Starter B (owner's pick, A77): sing = trill, dance = bob-and-weave, charm = preen, pose = crest. The armed column is the tier-2 upgrade of those (trill gains the chest beat, bob-and-weave gains stomps and claps, preen becomes offering food, crest becomes the extensible frill).

Target reaction (same Zanco-like target in every cell, fires on the success frame): ears snap up, head tilt, tail swish, fringe flare, then a small mirror of the gesture (sing: head up and jaw moving; dance: bounce and tail wag; charm: head lowers to the giver; pose: stands taller, fringe flared), plus the gold ground ring. No emoji, no sparkle. The target bows its neck by a per-body amount (octopus: head low, as if drinking at the rock) so its face stays in the close frame; it lifts the head a little when it reacts.

## Weakest cells (honest)
- The armed body is the least attractive rig (lumpy sausage torso, thick tubular arms); the gestures read, the creature does not look like a finished starter B.
- Octopus SING has no voice by design: it reads through bubbles, ripples and the pale mantle pulse, but a viewer who does not expect a silent song may miss it; the web is a flat triangle skirt that can look like a sail when an arm is raised (it is shortened for raised arms).
- The target is mostly only head, neck and legs in the close frame for the winged, octopus and snake columns (by the 40 % performer rule); use `T` for "both" or "close on target".
- Snake SING hiss breath is soft white balls, not a convincing spray; the sound arcs are secondary.
- The bird's folded wings look like a stack of planks from the side.

## Real-animal inspiration (from memory, unchecked)
- **Cobra hood**: many cobras rear the front third of the body and spread the neck ribs into a hood facing the threat, with a spectacle mark on some species (Naja). Hissing and a swaying head are part of the display.
- **Frilled lizard** (Chlamydosaurus): the collar frill opens like an umbrella, mouth wide, mostly a threat display rather than courtship.
- **Birds of paradise and other display birds**: wings and tail plumes spread and shimmered, hops and bobs on a cleared perch or arena; manakins hop and snap wings in rhythm; many birds present a gift (bowerbirds, some terns bring fish) or preen to show plumage.
- **Cuttlefish and octopus colour displays**: chromatophores make travelling dark bands ("passing cloud" in cuttlefish), pulsing patterns and spots; octopuses also raise arms and flatten the web in threat or display. Octopuses pulse the siphon and mantle when breathing; there is no voice.
- **Gorilla chest beat**: alternating open-hand strikes on the chest, with a vocal hoot; here the armed creature drums its chest while it trills.
- **Rattlesnake**: a rattle shaken in bursts with a hiss; the body coils and the head lifts in an S.
- **Gift giving and grooming** (primates, some birds, octopus offering shells in captivity anecdotes): offering objects with hands or beak, gentle touches with the arm tips.
